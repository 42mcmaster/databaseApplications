---

## What You'll Be Able To Do

1. Explain the difference between **data** and **information**
2. Explain what a **DBMS** does and why a spreadsheet isn't one
3. **Identify** common types of database and what each is built for
4. **Compare** database structures — flat file, hierarchical, relational
5. Name the **parts** of a database — table, record, field, key, schema
6. Describe what **SQL** is and what it's for
7. Describe how data gets **into** and **out of** a database
8. Explain why **integrity** and **security** matter
9. Tell a **front-end** from a **back-end**, and identify forms, filters and reports
10. Explain how **machine learning and neural networks** differ from decision trees, and discuss the ethics
11. Describe **careers** in databases and the credentials that support them

*State competencies: 2.8.1–2.8.9 · 2.14.1, 2.14.2 · 1.1.1, 1.1.2*

## What's in each segment

**1a** — You'll match eight real-world systems to the database type that fits them, compare two data structures, and take a side in a class discussion with no single right answer.

**1b** — Open `nba_5seasons.db` in DB Browser for SQLite and read its structure — no SQL yet. You'll inventory the tables, look closely at columns and types, find the primary keys (including two that need more than one column), and trace how tables connect to each other.

**1c** — Pick an app you actually use and take it apart: what's front-end, what's back-end, where's the form, the filter, the report. Then eight questions where the real skill isn't picking the right answer — it's explaining why the other three are wrong.

All of the actual items, questions, and tables you fill in are in **`unit1_AnswerSheet.md`**, organized by segment in the same order as above.
